# Math pack v4 — written, measured, REJECTED (2026-10-29)

Owner's brief: «substance over mechanism, leniency over strictness». Measured on the direct API
(owner-approved) against the registered bar (RUNLOG 2026-10-29). Production keeps pack **v3**.

* **Planner v4 → K1.** «Give every multi-step credit that partial» made Opus invent a partial for
  HALF an equation; `30x = 1000` (an invalid model) priced 1.75/7. Follow-through misses unchanged.
* **Verifier v4 (on the production plan) → K1 on the held-out set.** «In doubt, the higher option»
  credited the reasoning of a student who chose the wrong graph for the wrong reason (q5.א,
  1.5/5.75). Smoke and LaTeX smoke: identical to v3 (no gain).

Lesson for v5: leniency that is not anchored to a VALID choice leaks credit into wrong answers.
A doubt rule must be scoped to legibility/notation, never to the decision a check judges.

## planner (rejected)
```
- STEPS AS CHECKS. Each step the teacher names, or her solution shows as a separate result, is its own credit; a
  part checks only the work it asks for — a value from an earlier part is an input, never re-checked.
- ALTERNATIVE METHODS. Describe the milestones every valid method reaches, never one method's steps.
- OPERATIONS, NOT RESULTS. Describe a step by its operation on its inputs, never by the number it should produce
  («the distance is speed × time», not «150 km»).
- PARTIALS NEED A VALID METHOD. A partial is a valid method with a slip (calculation, copying, sign, rounding, an
  unfinished last step); an invalid method (a wrong operation, model or equation) is the zero option even when a
  number coincides. Give every multi-step credit that partial.
- FOLLOW-THROUGH. The correct operation on the student's own earlier value is the step's full option when that value
  came from a valid method with a slip; a value from an invalid method earns nothing in the steps built on it.
- SUBSTANCE OVER FORM. A result accepts every equivalent form (name them in the equivalence note); charge no form,
  layout, label or unit the teacher does not. Write math as \( \) LaTeX copied from the rubric; keep labels short.
```

## verifier (rejected)
```
- THE ANSWER IS A TRANSCRIPTION of her handwriting. Its mathematics is LaTeX inside \( \): quote a formula exactly
  as written there (\frac{1}{e}, never 1/e). [?] is ink that could not be read; «איור» lines read her drawing.
- SUBSTANCE OVER FORM. Any correct equivalent form of a result is the result (unsimplified, fraction or decimal,
  sensibly rounded, interval or inequality) unless the check names the form; charge no unnamed form, label or unit.
- VALID METHOD FIRST. A wrong formula, operation, model or relation takes the zero option even when a number or the
  final answer coincides. Whether a method is valid is never a matter of doubt.
- FOLLOW-THROUGH, FROM A VALID METHOD ONLY. When a step uses HER earlier value from a valid method with a slip
  (arithmetic, copying, sign, rounding), judge the operation on her value: a correct operation earns the step, and
  the slip is charged once, where it occurred. A value from an invalid method earns nothing in the steps after it.
- A PART IS JUDGED ON ITS OWN WORK; earlier values are inputs. A routine step done mentally is done when her next
  line uses its result correctly. IN DOUBT, THE HIGHER OPTION: when her valid work fits two options equally, or a
  mark may be a misreading, choose the higher; a FAULT needs a mistake plainly in her work. Never re-solve or correct it.
```

## explainer (not adopted with the pack)
```
- Point to the step and the value that went wrong. When a step earned credit on a value carried
  from an earlier error, name it as follow-through.
- Write mathematics inside \( \) exactly as the answer writes it (\frac{1}{e}, not 1/e); never $...$ or \[ \].
```
